The Unshakable Devotee

A daily worshipper of Ram faces mockery, yet one day a miracle silences the skeptics.

Story

I want you to picture a small town tucked between rolling hills, where the mornings are scented with jasmine and the evenings hum with cicada song. In the heart of this town lived a man named Arvind. Every sunrise, before the rooster even cracked its first note, Arvind would rise, fold his thin blanket, and walk barefoot to a modest shrine at the edge of his courtyard. There he placed a single flower, lit a small oil lamp, and whispered the name of Ram, his voice steady as the mountain wind. He repeated the same verses, the same prayers, day after day, as if his very breath were a prayer itself.

Now, Arvind was not a wealthy man. He owned a tiny shop that sold spices, and his earnings barely covered his family’s needs. Yet his devotion never wavered. The townspeople watched him, some with admiration, many with a smirk. They would gather at the tea stall after work and joke, "Look at Arvind, praying to a stone while we chase profit!" They teased him, calling his daily ritual a superstition, a waste of time. Children would mimic his gestures, pretending to bow to an invisible deity, and the laughter would echo down the narrow lanes.

Arvind never answered with anger. He would simply smile, pat the head of the child who mocked him, and say, "Ram sees all, even the jokes we make. He knows the heart behind the laughter." The jokes continued, growing louder with each passing season. Some even suggested that Arvind’s devotion was a show, a way to attract sympathy. Yet he persisted, his faith a quiet river that refused to be dammed by ridicule.

One sweltering summer afternoon, the town was hit by a sudden storm. Dark clouds rolled in like an angry sea, and thunder rumbled far off. The river that ran through the outskirts of town swelled with rain, breaking its banks and rushing toward the low‑lying streets. Panic spread as people scrambled to protect their homes. The market stalls were packed with frantic shoppers, and the tea stall—usually a place of chatter—was silent, save for the howl of the wind.

Arvind, hearing the cries, ran to his shrine. He knelt, eyes closed, and whispered a plea to Ram, "Protect our town, protect those who have no shelter, protect those who have mocked my devotion. Let your love be the shield that holds us together." He lit the lamp with trembling hands, the flame dancing against the gusts.

As the water surged, a massive boulder—part of an ancient temple wall that had long stood abandoned—rolled down from the hill. It seemed destined to smash into the narrow lane where families had gathered, a lane that would become a river of destruction. In that heartbeat, something extraordinary happened. The boulder halted, not a foot from the crowd, as if an invisible hand had pressed against it. The water that had been roaring forward fell back, swirling around the stone, then receded.

The townspeople stared, mouths agape, as the storm began to lose its fury. The rain softened to a drizzle, and the wind became a gentle sigh. The river, which had threatened to drown their homes, settled into a calm trickle. A hush fell over the crowd, and then a single voice whispered, "Ram..."

One by one, the skeptics fell to their knees, their faces wet with rain and tears. The child who had mimicked Arvind’s bow now bowed for real, his eyes wide with awe. The tea stall owner, who had led the jokes, placed a cup of hot tea at the foot of the shrine, his hands shaking. He looked at Arvind and said, "Your devotion saved us. We were blind to the love you carried.

Arvind rose, his simple cloth shirt damp, and simply replied, "It was never my strength that halted the stone. It was the love of Ram that resides in every sincere heart. When we pray, we become vessels for that love, and love moves mountains, or in this case, boulders."

From that day onward, the town changed. The jokes faded, replaced by reverent silence at sunrise. People began to join Arvind at his shrine, offering flowers, lighting lamps, and speaking the name of Ram with a newfound respect. The story of that storm traveled far, reminding all who heard it that devotion, when pure, is a quiet power that can calm the fiercest of storms and turn mockery into reverence. And Arvind? He continued his daily worship, not because he sought applause, but because his heart had always known that the divine walks beside him, steady as the sunrise, patient as the river, and ever‑present in the simple act of devotion.
